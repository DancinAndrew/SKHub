# Analysis framework

## Evidence hierarchy

Weight evidence by discriminatory power, not by how emotionally vivid it sounds.

### Higher weight

- repeated behavior across multiple sober dates;
- costly initiative: reopening contact, scheduling, calling, sustained attention, concrete follow-through;
- spontaneous relationship-history inquiry or present-fit evaluation;
- behavior that persists after an intoxicated or emotionally intense episode;
- clear refusal, boundary, or non-response to a relationship frame;
- consistent changes in initiative, reciprocity, medium, or future planning.

### Medium weight

- personal disclosure and reliable emotional support;
- repeated daily-update expectations;
- sober praise, embarrassment, flirtatious role play, or special-treatment language;
- one-on-one future ideas that receive detail and follow-through;
- mutual rituals such as good morning/good night when both parties maintain them.

### Lower weight alone

- raw message count;
- response speed;
- emoji, reaction, heart, sticker, or one compliment;
- one drunken conversation;
- accepting a plan proposed entirely by the other person;
- a call with a functional reason such as driving or boredom;
- third-party teasing;
- a single ambiguous phrase without surrounding context.

## Attribution rules

Always separate:

1. **Initiation**: who first created the topic, call opportunity, plan, or romantic frame.
2. **Acceptance**: who merely agreed or did not refuse.
3. **Extension**: who added details, cost, timing, or emotional meaning.
4. **Execution**: who actually called, booked, met, sent, or followed through.
5. **Repair**: who returned after silence, corrected a missed ritual, or restored continuity.

For calls, reconstruct the full event sequence. “Proposed a call,” “attempted a call,” “missed call,” “called back,” and “completed a call” are different facts.

## Behavioral coding

Code every substantive session for:

- conversation initiation and reopening;
- follow-up questions;
- state and daily-life updates;
- update expectations;
- emotional disclosure, soothing, and practical care;
- humor and shared language;
- appearance or competence praise;
- embarrassment, ambiguity, avoidance, and topic shifts;
- jealousy or attention competition;
- relationship-position testing;
- former partners and other romantic targets;
- future meetings and joint activity;
- gifts, money, and practical effort;
- greeting rituals;
- media and call escalation;
- cooling, refusal, boundary setting, and low investment;
- reciprocity and asymmetry.

Do not turn code counts directly into romantic conclusions. Interpret function and context.

## Competing models

Evaluate at least:

- ordinary friends;
- familiar former partners;
- emotional-support companions;
- enjoying attention without romantic intent;
- mutual flirtation;
- renewed romantic attraction;
- feelings without commitment readiness;
- active reconciliation tendency.

For each model, state:

- what it explains well;
- what it cannot explain without strain;
- evidence that would lower or raise it;
- whether it explains intimacy, attraction, and commitment equally well.

## State distinctions

Keep these separate:

- enjoying conversation;
- enjoying attention;
- familiarity and historical safety;
- special emotional importance;
- emotional dependence;
- flirtation and ambiguity;
- romantic or physical attraction;
- renewed liking;
- willingness to increase real-world contact;
- willingness to define a formal relationship.

High intimacy does not imply high commitment. A former relationship can raise familiarity and lower boundaries without proving reconciliation.

## Alcohol and emotional peaks

Do not automatically treat intoxicated statements as hidden truth or meaningless noise.

Check:

- what changed while intoxicated;
- whether sober behavior later confirms, acknowledges, minimizes, or withdraws it;
- whether access, loneliness, time-zone availability, or safety needs explain the behavior;
- whether the same pattern recurs while sober.

## Media

Interpret media by its conversational function:

- information sharing;
- routine update;
- emotional reaction;
- avoidance or embarrassment;
- joke;
- intimacy cue;
- unknown.

Use neighboring messages and repeated usage. Do not infer unknown audio or inaccessible media content.

## Probability calibration

Use ranges only after the full analysis. Label them subjective behavioral estimates.

For each range, include:

- confidence level;
- strongest support;
- strongest counterevidence;
- unknown facts that could materially change the estimate.

An independent reviewer should recalibrate the ranges without seeing earlier subagent conclusions. Prefer wider or lower ranges when the data window is short, unusually intense, intoxication-heavy, or dominated by one party’s framing.
